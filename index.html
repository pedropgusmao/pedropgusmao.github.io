About

I am a Lecturer in Computer Science at the University of Surrey, where I am part of the {{ site.author.group }}. My research is on federated learning, machine unlearning, and privacy-preserving machine learning more broadly.

Before joining Surrey, I was a Senior Research Associate at the University of Cambridge, where I was one of the core developers of Flower, the open-source federated learning framework. Before that, I was a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Oxford, working on localisation and navigation for emergency responders. I hold a PhD from Politecnico di Torino and a BSc in Electrical Engineering from the University of São Paulo.

Research

Federated learning
Training models across many devices or organisations without moving their data: aggregation, heterogeneous clients, semi-supervised and self-supervised settings, and the systems that make this run at scale.
Machine unlearning
Removing the influence of specific data from trained models, with guarantees that can be checked, including in federated settings where no single party holds the training set.
Privacy and security
Secure aggregation, robustness to malicious participants, and privacy-enhancing technologies for collaborative learning.
